# Bounded noncanonical-encoding checks

**Targeted optimization checks.** The five named examples in article Sections III-A--III-C were checked by arbitrary-encoding enumeration at $h\in\{0,1/2,1,3/2,2,32\}$. The original syntax and its ordered-ranked translation were enumerated separately. Every nonconstant input subterm was eligible as an expansion, including nonshared subterms and multiple names for the same expansion. Each name retained a separate identity, and every matching reference or inline choice was allowed at output positions, including output roots, and proper definition-body positions. Body roots remained constructors. Equal-expansion name permutations may be counted repeatedly. These are targeted inputs, not an exhaustive universe through 17 nodes.

**Size bound and independence.** For an input of $N$ nodes and $r$ roots, the targeted enumeration used at most $\lfloor(N-r)/2\rfloor$ definitions and at most $N$ stored syntax nodes. A legal body contains at least two nodes and an output at least one. Since definition charges are nonnegative, any encoding with more than $N$ stored nodes is more expensive than the unshared representation. Thus the fixed size cutoff excludes no optimum; it does not assume canonicalization, candidate restriction or duplicate-definition merging. Unreachable definitions were omitted because deleting them strictly reduces stored syntax. In the acyclic dependency graph, all definitions are reachable precisely when the union of references from bodies and outputs contains every name.

**Preflight and execution.** Before evaluating charged costs or canonical minima, a preflight counted each fixture's admitted encodings using stored-size and reference-mask states. The limits were 120 seconds and 2,000,000 combined source/target encodings per fixture; all five counts completed. The scope and code hashes were then recorded before validation, with a 180-second limit per fixture. Validation explicitly visited all 9,972 counted encodings; no fixture remained incomplete. Each visited encoding was expanded and checked for the forward expansion/translation identity or cost-preserving reverse erasure. The source and target minima agreed with both all-subterm and shared-subterm canonical minima in all 30 example--charge pairs. The forest formula was compared only at the three charges at most one. Strict savings occurred in 23 of the 30 pairs. The two fixtures with adjacent optional omissions are the chain and L1.

**Larger translation fixtures.** The previously completed nine-node unordered and ten-node mixed fixtures each contain two copies of a four-node unary subtree. Each generated 210 source and 210 target encodings and passed six charge comparisons and 420 mapping checks. Their minima are respectively $(7,7.5,8,8.5,9,9)$ and $(8,8.5,9,9.5,10,10)$ in the charge order above. Both have strict savings at the first four charges. Their shared subtree has three body edges and lies outside the optional unary forest, so these fixtures test profitable selection and translation rather than adjacency between optional omissions.

An additional twelve-node fixture uses two roots, r1(q(g(a),k(b))) and r2(q(k(b),g(a))), where q is unordered and the other constructors are ordered. Counting completed before optimization: 46 source and 19 target encodings were admitted under the same fixed syntax-size bound. All 65 encodings passed mapping and cost checks; arbitrary source, arbitrary target and both canonical minima agreed at all six charges. The minima are (9, 9.5, 10, 10.5, 11, 12), with strict savings at the first five charges, matching min(12,9+h). The forest formula also agrees at the first three charges, but this fixture tests permuted profitable sharing rather than optional-forest adjacency. The preflight, frozen hashes and run are recorded separately in permuted_results; each phase had a 120-second limit and a 2,000,000-encoding cap.

**Small-universe sanity and translation checks.** The original exhaustive universe uses two constants, one ordered unary symbol, one ordered binary symbol, a positive-arity unordered unranked symbol, and a positive-arity mixed symbol with one ordered prefix position and an exchangeable suffix (possibly empty). Languages are sets under source tree equality, including the empty language. The size bound counts expanded nodes of distinct roots. Preflights at bounds 4, 5 and 6 used 120 seconds and a 200,000 combined-encoding cap. Bounds 4 and 5 completed; bound 6 stopped at 200,001 encodings and is excluded from the completed scope. Bound 5 was frozen before objective comparisons. It comprises 1,761 languages, 10,637 source encodings and 9,186 target encodings: 10,566 language--charge comparisons and 19,823 mapping checks completed without mismatch. This original enumeration used the definition-count bound but no stored-size or incumbent-cost pruning.

Every minimum in the five-node universe equals the unshared cost at the six tested charges, so its optimization role is checker sanity rather than demonstration of profitable sharing. Its translation checks include 990 generated source encodings across 187 languages with at least one direct reference child and two distinct expanded child classes in the same exchangeable group: 894 unordered cases and 96 mixed cases. These counts include repeated presentations and equal-expansion name permutations; they are not 990 independent inputs. A separate census based on raw presentations, recursive isomorphism matching and generating-polynomial counting reproduced the language counts $1,2,7,41,236,1474$ at total sizes zero through five.

**Cross-checks and limits.** The targeted size-pruned generator was compared as a full encoding set with the earlier literal generator, filtered by the same stored-size bound, on all 1,761 small languages and both syntax forms. All 3,522 comparisons agreed, covering 3,918 admitted encodings. Boundary fixtures also test permuted equal roots, multiplicities, fixed prefixes, inline/reference mixtures, whole-root references and duplicate definitions; an incorrect spelling-sorted translation is rejected. The enumeration and canonical search use different algorithms but share term, expansion and size helpers; the generator comparison also shares its declared domain. Consequently these checks are internal finite implementation evidence, not independent proof certification. Translation checks cover the admitted encodings, not every legal encoding of arbitrary size. They do not cover every input or rational charge, and the hardness reductions were not tested.

**Reproduction.** The companion directory `analysis/bounded_encodings/` contains the code, protocols and raw records. The five inputs are explicit in `targeted.py` and in the JSON results under `targeted_results/`. From that directory run `python targeted.py count L1 new_count.json`, then `python targeted.py run L1 new_run.json --seconds 180`; replace L1 with demand13, chain16, L2 or L3 for the other fixtures. Run `python targeted_crosscheck.py new_crosscheck.json` for the small-universe generator comparison and translation composition count. Output paths must not exist. The original commands remain `python check.py run --bound 5 --seconds 600 --out new_result.json`, `python census.py new_census.json`, `python fixtures.py new_fixtures.json` and `python profitable_fixtures.py new_profitable`. The targeted scope, preflight and run records identify code hashes; the original checker retains SHA-256 `dbfd0135ff2c7fa766ef50cbb5bdb85aaf18a1d5cbb6bef546c6349ba5ca12ea`. These checks add no performance observations to the main experiments.



**Public-package reproduction.** From the repository root, run `python -B analysis/bounded_encodings/reproduce_checks.py new_checks`. This reruns the bounded checks and compares deterministic fields with the archived records. These finite checks correspond to the article subsection Correctness checks and reproduction and are stored in `analysis/bounded_encodings/`; repository result table S7 is the separate `results/tables/S7_all_conditions.csv` execution roster.
